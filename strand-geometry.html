E4 Decision Tree: Circle Theorems, Similarity & Polygons

When a CIE geometry question says "Give a geometrical reason for each step", quote the exact theorem names shown below to earn every B1 reason mark.

1. Circle Theorems & Symmetry (Exact Wording)

  • Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
  • Angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$; Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$.
  • Tangent is perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the radius at the point of contact.
  • Tangents from an external point are equal in length.
  • Alternate Segment Theorem: angle between tangent and chord equals angle in the alternate segment.
  • Perpendicular from centre to a chord bisects the chord; equal chords are equidistant from the centre.

2. Similar Shapes ($k$, $k^2$, $k^3$)

  • Length ratio: $1 : k$
  • Area / Surface area ratio: $1 : k^2$
  • Volume / Mass / Capacity ratio: $1 : k^3$
  • Always return to the length ratio $k$ first when converting between Area and Volume ($\text{Area} \xrightarrow{\sqrt{\phantom{x}}} \text{Length} \xrightarrow{(\phantom{x})^3} \text{Volume}$).

3. Polygons & Congruence Properties

  • Exterior angle sum of ANY convex polygon $= 360^\circ$. For a regular $n$-gon, $\text{Ext} = \frac{360^\circ}{n}$ and $\text{Int} + \text{Ext} = 180^\circ$.
  • Interior angle sum $= (n - 2) \times 180^\circ$.
  • Congruent shapes: Identical shape and size (all corresponding sides and angles are equal).

4. Bearings & 3D Symmetry

  • Bearings: Always 1) measured from North, 2) clockwise, 3) written as 3 digits (e.g. $042^\circ$).
  • Back-bearing: Add or subtract $180^\circ$ (using co-interior angles on parallel North lines).
  • Planes of symmetry: Count both longitudinal planes through the cross-section AND the transverse slice halfway along a prism.

High-Frequency CIE Examiner Traps

  • Forgetting to subtract the small triangle in parallel-line similarity: If $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle ADE$, the area scale factor gives $\text{Area}(\triangle ADE)$, NOT the trapezium $BCED$. Always subtract $\text{Area}(\triangle ABC)$.
  • "Z-angle" or "F-angle" slang: Examiners award $0$ marks for "Z-angles". Always write alternate angles, corresponding angles, or co-interior (allied) angles.
  • Assuming a cyclic quadrilateral when a vertex is at the centre $O$: Only 4 points on the circumference form a cyclic quadrilateral. If one vertex is the centre $O$, use "angle at centre = $2 \times$ angle at circumference" instead.
