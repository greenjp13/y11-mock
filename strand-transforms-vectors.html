E7 Decision Tree: Full Transformation Descriptions & Vector Geometry

When asked to "Describe fully the single transformation...", naming two transformations scores 0 marks. Every transformation type has a strict checklist of required details.

1. Transformation Description Checklist

  • Translation + column vector $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ (2 marks).
  • Reflection + equation of mirror line, e.g. $y = x$ or $x = -1$ (2 marks).
  • Rotation + angle ($90^\circ / 180^\circ$) + direction (clockwise/anticlockwise, not needed for $180^\circ$) + centre $(a, b)$ (3 marks).
  • Enlargement + scale factor $k$ + centre $(a, b)$ (3 marks).

2. Coordinate Rules & Negative Scale Factors

  • $90^\circ$ anticlockwise about $(0,0)$: $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$.
  • $90^\circ$ clockwise about $(0,0)$: $(x, y) \to (y, -x)$.
  • $180^\circ$ about $(0,0)$: $(x, y) \to (-x, -y)$.
  • Enlargement centre $(a, b)$, scale factor $k$: $\text{Image} = \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} + k\begin{pmatrix} x - a \\ y - b \end{pmatrix}$.

3. Vector Arithmetic, Magnitude & Ratios

  • Position vector rule: $\vec{AB} = \vec{OB} - \vec{OA} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$.
  • Magnitude (length): $\left|\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}\right| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ (always positive).
  • Ratio division: If $AP : PB = m : n$, then $\vec{AP} = \dfrac{m}{m + n}\vec{AB}$ (denominator is $m + n$, NOT $n$!).

4. Proving Parallelism & Collinearity

  • Parallel: Show one vector is a scalar multiple of the other ($\vec{PQ} = k\vec{RS}$).
  • Collinear (straight line): Show $\vec{PQ} = k\vec{PR}$ (parallel) AND state that they share the common point $P$.

High-Frequency CIE Examiner Traps

  • Writing a coordinate $(x, y)$ instead of a column vector $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ for a translation: Examiners strictly penalise $(3, -2)$ for a translation—always write $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$.
  • Omitting "common point" in collinearity proofs: Showing $\vec{BC} = 2\vec{AB}$ only proves the segments are parallel. You MUST explicitly write "and they share the common point $B$" to earn the final mark.
  • Vector arrows & bolding: In handwritten work, always underline vector letters ($\underline{a}$ or $\mathbf{a}$) or draw an arrow ($\vec{AB}$) so they are not confused with scalar lengths.
